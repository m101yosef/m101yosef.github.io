## The problem

TStore's product details page — the page a shopper lands on right before deciding to buy — was the focus of this project: does it give someone enough information to decide quickly, and does it make them want to come back.

## Constraints

This was UX research and redesign work on an existing page, not a rebuilt storefront — the deliverable was a reworked product details page and the reasoning behind it, evaluated against usability principles rather than live sales data.

## What I did

I reviewed the existing page against what actually drives a purchase decision at that stage — clear product information, trust signals, and an easy path to checkout — and reworked the layout and content around those priorities rather than around what was easiest to build. Loyalty was treated as part of the same problem: a page that's clear and trustworthy the first time is also the one a customer is willing to come back to.

## What I'd credit

The output was a redesigned product details page, not a measured lift in sales or repeat purchases — I don't have TStore's data to point to. It's a small, early example of a pattern I keep coming back to: good UX and good business outcomes are usually the same design problem, described from two different angles.
